Communication Protocols in BMS and Industrial Integration

Throughout my professional career, I’ve worked on projects where integration between devices, sensors and platforms is key. In this context, understanding and mastering the main communication protocols in the market is essential to ensure compatibility, scalability and performance.

Here’s a summary of the most relevant ones in building automation (BMS), industry and smart control:

🔌 Modbus#

One of the most widespread protocols in industrial automation. Simple, robust and well supported. I’ve worked with Modbus RTU over RS485 and Modbus TCP over IP networks for HVAC, energy and lighting control systems.

🏢 BACnet#

A standard protocol in BMS environments. I’ve used it in HVAC and air quality systems. Enables structured communication between devices from different manufacturers.

🔄 M-Bus#

Used for remote reading of utility meters (water, gas, electricity). I’ve integrated it into residential buildings and centralized consumption platforms.

🌐 OPC-UA#

A powerful and modern industrial protocol. Object-oriented and secure, ideal for Industry 4.0 and SCADA integration.

💡 DALI#

Digital lighting control protocol. I’ve implemented DALI in energy-efficiency solutions for office buildings and public spaces.

📶 Zigbee and Z-Wave#

Wireless protocols used mainly in home automation: sensors, lighting, blinds, etc. Easy to integrate into platforms like Home Assistant.

☁️ MQTT#

Lightweight and reliable for IoT communications. I’ve used MQTT in both home and industrial setups, connected to brokers like Mosquitto and platforms such as Node-RED and FIWARE.

🔧 Other protocols I’ve worked with:#

  • KNX: European standard for smart buildings
  • LoRaWAN: long-range, low-power communications
  • REST / JSON / WebSocket: for cloud and API integrations
  • SNMP: for network and infrastructure monitoring
  • LonWorks: used in legacy BMS installations

🎯 Conclusion#

Each protocol has its own scope and strengths. The key is to select the right one for each environment and know how to combine them efficiently.

I’ve designed architectures where several of these protocols coexist, always focusing on reliability, interoperability, and long-term scalability.
